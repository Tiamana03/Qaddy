# Qaddy Founder Blueprint
Version: 1.0  
Status: Active  
Last Updated: September 2026

---

# Purpose

This document defines the mission, vision, philosophy and long-term direction of Qaddy.

Every product decision, design choice, engineering decision and business opportunity should be measured against this blueprint.

If a decision does not support the mission of Qaddy, it should not be pursued.

---

# Our Mission

To help every golfer enjoy the game more by bringing every part of their golfing life together in one beautifully designed experience.

Golf is more than eighteen holes.

It is friendships.

Travel.

Achievements.

Competition.

Learning.

Memories.

Qaddy exists to connect all of those experiences into one place.

---

# Our Vision

To become the world's most loved golf platform.

When golfers think about their game, they think about Qaddy.

Not simply because it records scores, but because it helps create better golfing experiences.

---

# Why Qaddy Exists

Today's golf experience is fragmented.

Golfers use multiple apps to:

- Track scores
- Record statistics
- Manage their golf bag
- Organise trips
- Find courses
- Message friends
- Book tee times
- Learn their game
- Record achievements

Every app solves one problem.

None solve the golfer's life.

Qaddy changes that.

---

# Our North Star

Every round has a story.

Qaddy exists to help golfers remember it.

---

# Who We Build For

Qaddy is built for golfers who genuinely love the game.

They enjoy:

- Playing regularly
- Improving
- Travelling
- Sharing memories
- Discovering courses
- Competing with friends
- Building a golfing legacy

Whether someone plays five rounds a year or one hundred, they should feel welcomed.

---

# Product Philosophy

Every feature must answer at least one of these questions:

Does it help golfers:

- Play better?
- Connect with others?
- Remember more?
- Discover somewhere new?
- Improve their game?
- Enjoy golf more?

If the answer is no, reconsider building it.

# Decision Framework

Before building any feature, ask:

1. Does this improve the golfer's experience?
2. Is it simple?
3. Is it beautiful?
4. Will golfers still value this in five years?
5. Does it align with our mission?
6. Would we proudly demonstrate this feature to every golfer?

If the answer to any of these is no, rethink the solution.
